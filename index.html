The recruitment funnel

Publicly discovers campaign→Volunteer page→Signup→Follow-up→Orientation→First activity→Active volunteer

Broad public channels

  • Campaign website with a prominent Volunteer button
  • 3–5 short public videos per week
  • Public event and activity updates
  • Permitted information tables and community events
  • QR codes on lawful campaign materials
  • Existing volunteers sharing the same public signup page

Keep signup simple

  • Name and contact information
  • General availability
  • Preferred role
  • Whether they can attend orientation
  • Assign a concrete first activity immediately after onboarding

Month-by-month plan

SEPTEMBER
18 → 35

Build the machine. Create one database, one signup route, appoint 2–3 reliable coordinators, start weekly orientation and establish the content rhythm.

OCTOBER
35 → 60

Scale without chaos. Add team leads, keep public activity consistent and move every legitimate signup toward a first activity.

NOVEMBER
60 → 85

Focus heavily on retention. Identify inactive volunteers, check availability, improve onboarding and keep recurring people scheduled.

DECEMBER
85 → 100–150

Continue broad recruitment while protecting retention. Build capacity above 100 because volunteer availability changes week to week.

Team structure

Volunteer roles

  • Registered petition canvassers, once approved
  • Events and public information
  • Administration and data
  • Volunteer onboarding
  • Logistics and material delivery
  • Photography/video and communications
  • Team coordinators

Coordinator rule

Aim for roughly one coordinator for every 8–12 active volunteers as the team grows. The campaign manager should eventually manage coordinators and systems rather than trying to personally manage 100+ people.

Donations and compliance

Keep fundraising and spending controlled by the registered financial agent or authorized assistant financial agent.

Use one official donation page, link to it from the campaign website and public profiles, and use a QR code at permitted events. Do not have ordinary volunteers casually collect campaign money. Paid or boosted political advertising may trigger Elections BC advertising requirements, so verify the rules before spending on promotion.

This page is an operations plan, not legal advice. Confirm current recall, canvassing, advertising and financing requirements directly with Elections BC before acting.

Training rules to build in

  • Only properly registered canvassers may collect recall signatures.
  • Do not collect signatures before registration and required identification are in place.
  • Petition sheets must be handled according to Elections BC requirements.
  • Permission may be required for malls, recreation centres, strata property, apartment buildings and other facilities.
  • Follow local bylaws, property rules and current Elections BC instructions.
